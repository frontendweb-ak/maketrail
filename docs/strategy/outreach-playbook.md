# Market validation outreach playbook

**Status:** Proposed fieldwork aid; outreach not yet recorded  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-25  
**Depends on:** [30-day market validation plan](market-validation.md) · [Market validation operating kit](validation-operations.md)

## Purpose and boundary

This document helps recruit relevant buyer and creator interview participants. It does not change the target counts, hypotheses, interview prompts, or decision gates in the parent plan.

**No outreach is represented as sent.** Do not add a person or organization to the participant tracker until a real prospect has been identified and a genuine outreach attempt has occurred. Do not claim a response, meeting, commitment, or endorsement without evidence.

## 1. Prospect selection

### Buyer prospects

Prioritize development agencies that show public evidence of:
- Delivering custom web or software projects for multiple clients.
- Repeated work in SaaS, business applications, commerce, internal tools, or similar product categories.
- A technical delivery function with an owner, CTO, engineering manager, or technical lead.
- A plausible reason to reuse modules across projects.

Avoid selecting prospects solely because they are large, famous, or have a generic “technology” label. Prefer a relevant delivery profile over company size.

### Creator prospects

Prioritize independent developers or small studios with public evidence of:
- A reusable software product, library, starter, module, or integration they appear to have created.
- Public documentation, demo, release history, or a working example.
- Clear authorship/ownership signals or a credible route to establish distribution rights.
- A stated maintenance or support channel.
- Potential interest in commercial distribution or implementation services.

A public repository, package, or product page is a lead signal—not proof of ownership, license rights, security, quality, or willingness to participate.

### Exclusion and caution rules

- Do not approach people using scraped personal contact data or deceptive identities.
- Do not infer private contact details or bypass access controls.
- Do not include minors or request sensitive personal information.
- Do not ask prospects to share confidential client code, credentials, or private documents.
- Do not imply a partnership, guaranteed sales, investment, endorsement, or launch date.
- Do not promise marketplace traffic, revenue, verification, or legal/payment capabilities that have not been established.

## 2. Sourcing channels

Use public, permission-respecting sources and record the broad source category in the participant tracker.

| Segment | Candidate channels | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers | Agency directories, agency websites, professional communities, founder/engineering networks, referrals, relevant events | Multi-project delivery, technical decision-makers, repeated capability needs |
| Creators | Public package registries, open-source communities, product directories, developer communities, indie-maker communities, referrals | Original reusable work, documentation, maintenance, clear licensing signals |

Start with warm introductions where available. For cold outreach, use a public business contact channel intended for inquiries. Respect platform rules, unsubscribe requests, and applicable anti-spam/privacy requirements. Do not automate bulk unsolicited messages.

## 3. Outreach sequence

1. Identify a relevant public business/product profile and record the source privately.
2. Assign a research ID and add only non-identifying metadata to the participant tracker.
3. Send one short, individualized invitation explaining why their experience is relevant.
4. If there is no response, at most one polite follow-up after a reasonable interval (suggested: 5–7 business days).
5. If declined or asked not to be contacted, record the status and stop.
6. If accepted, agree on purpose, duration, format, consent, and whether recording/quotation is requested.
7. Conduct the interview using the existing prompts in the parent validation plan; do not pitch before understanding their current workflow.
8. Capture anonymized evidence promptly using the operating kit.

Suggested outreach is an invitation to research, not a sales pitch. Adapt it honestly; do not imply an existing product, customer, or traction.

## 4. Message templates

### Buyer invitation

> Subject: Short research conversation about reusable software in agency projects
>
> Hi,
>
> I’m researching how development agencies handle capabilities they repeatedly build or integrate across client projects—particularly the effort involved in evaluation, licensing, compatibility, and implementation.
>
> I’m looking to speak with agency owners and technical delivery leads for a 20–25 minute research conversation about a recent real project. This is discovery research, not a sales pitch, and I won’t ask for confidential client information.
>
> Would you be open to a short conversation? If useful, I can share the themes I’m investigating beforehand.
>
> Thanks.

### Creator invitation

> Subject: Research conversation about distributing reusable developer products
>
> Hi,
>
> I’m researching how independent developers and small studios distribute and support reusable software they have created, including licensing, compatibility information, buyer evaluation, and implementation services.
>
> I’d value a 20–25 minute conversation about one product you have actually built and maintained. This is discovery research, not a request to list your work or make a commitment. Please don’t share private code, credentials, or confidential customer information.
>
> Would you be open to a short conversation?
>
> Thanks.

### One follow-up

> Hi,
>
> Just following up once on my invitation to a short research conversation about your experience with [agency delivery / distributing reusable developer software]. If this isn’t relevant or the timing is poor, no reply is needed and I won’t keep following up.
>
> Thanks.

### Acceptance and consent confirmation

> Thanks for agreeing. The conversation will take about 20–25 minutes and focus on your experience with past projects. Participation is voluntary. I’ll take notes for product research and anonymize them in any shared summary. I will not record the conversation unless you explicitly agree beforehand. You can skip any question or stop at any time. I won’t request confidential client information, private source code, credentials, or payment details.

Do not use the acceptance message as a substitute for any consent or privacy notice required by applicable law or the chosen recording platform.

## 5. Screening before scheduling

Ask only enough to confirm relevance; avoid turning screening into a second interview.

### Buyer screen
- Does the organization deliver software for multiple clients or products?
- Does the person participate in technical delivery, architecture, procurement, or budget decisions?
- Can they discuss a recent project without revealing client-confidential information?
- Is there a repeated software capability or integration problem worth exploring?

### Creator screen
- Has the person or studio created a reusable software product they can discuss?
- Can they point to public documentation, a demo, or a release record?
- Are they able to explain authorship, ownership, and licensing?
- Can they describe maintenance and support expectations?

Screening answers are self-reported until independently supported. Do not qualify a creator or product solely from a screening response.

## 6. Scheduling and interview conduct

- Offer a short 20–25 minute conversation and use the participant's preferred reasonable time.
- Confirm timezone and meeting method; do not assume location from a profile.
- Start by explaining purpose, voluntary participation, note handling, and consent choices.
- Ask about the most recent concrete example before discussing Maketrail.
- Ask neutral follow-ups: “What happened next?”, “How do you know?”, “What did that cost?”, “What did you choose instead?”
- Avoid leading questions, inflated claims, and asking whether they “like the idea.”
- Close by asking whether a specific follow-up is welcome. Do not assume permission for future marketing or sales contact.

## 7. Recruitment quality and weekly review

Track the following internally, using only anonymized IDs in the public tracker:
- Prospects identified by segment and sourcing channel.
- Outreach attempts, replies, declines, and no-response counts.
- Interviews completed by segment and role band.
- Reasons for ineligibility or decline where voluntarily provided.
- Concentration by geography, agency size, technology stack, referral network, and channel.
- Whether participants are independent of one another or share the same ecosystem.

Review selection bias weekly. A set of friendly founder contacts or one developer community is not representative of the market. Seek disconfirming perspectives and participants who currently prefer internal builds or established alternatives.

## 8. Privacy and contact-data handling

- Store names and contact details only in an access-controlled private contact system.
- Keep public repository records limited to research IDs and non-identifying statuses.
- Do not publish contact lists, raw messages, email headers, recordings, transcripts, or identifiable quotes.
- Record consent scope and withdrawal requests in the private system.
- Stop outreach promptly when asked; do not transfer declined contacts into another campaign.
- Set a retention period for contact and interview materials and delete them when no longer needed.
- Use public professional contact channels only where appropriate and permitted; comply with applicable privacy and anti-spam rules.

## 9. Execution status

Actual progress belongs in the anonymized participant tracker in [validation-operations.md](validation-operations.md), with sensitive source material kept private.
