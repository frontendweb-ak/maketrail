# Maketrail product vision

**Status:** Working hypothesis  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-24

## One-line vision

Make useful software easier to discover, trust, acquire, and put to work.

## Mission

Help software creators turn reusable work into sustainable opportunities, while helping businesses and developers find and implement software solutions without repeatedly building everything from scratch.

## Problem hypothesis

Development agencies and software teams repeatedly implement common capabilities across client and internal projects. Existing discovery channels can make it difficult to judge whether a reusable solution is legitimate, maintained, compatible, appropriately licensed, and practical to integrate.

Independent developers and small studios may have useful reusable products but lack reliable distribution, buyer access, and a path to implementation revenue.

These are hypotheses to validate through interviews and observed transactions—not established market facts.

## Proposed solution

A marketplace organized around a complete buyer outcome:

**Discover → Evaluate → Purchase → Integrate**

A listing may combine:
- A reusable software product or module.
- Supported technologies, versions, dependencies, and environments.
- Demo, documentation, changelog, and known limitations.
- Explicit licensing and usage rights.
- Maintenance and support expectations.
- A clear verification level and evidence.
- Optional implementation, customization, and maintenance services.

## Initial target segments

**Primary buyer hypothesis:** development agencies that repeatedly deliver software projects and can identify recurring implementation needs.

**Secondary buyer hypothesis:** SaaS startups and software teams that need to accelerate delivery.

**Initial creator hypothesis:** independent developers and small software studios with reusable products they own and can support.

The product should be globally oriented and English-first. Payment availability, tax, sanctions, consumer protection, and compliance requirements may constrain where transactions can be offered.

## Initial product wedge hypothesis

Start with reusable software modules rather than attempting to launch a universal marketplace for every digital product.

Candidate areas to investigate:
- Authentication, authorization, and organization management.
- Payments, subscriptions, and billing integrations.
- Notifications and messaging.
- Admin dashboards and business operations modules.
- File management and document workflows.
- Reporting, audit logs, and business workflows.

Do not commit to these categories until buyer evidence identifies frequent, expensive, and purchasable needs.

## Differentiation hypothesis

Maketrail should compete on confidence and implementation outcomes, not catalog size alone:
1. Genuine creator and ownership information.
2. Clear licensing and compatibility.
3. Transparent verification evidence.
4. Relevant discovery based on buyer requirements.
5. Creator-led or qualified implementation services.
6. Buyer feedback tied to real usage and integration outcomes.

## Product principles

1. **Trust before volume:** curate carefully and represent verification honestly.
2. **Outcome over download:** help buyers get solutions working.
3. **Creator rights:** respect ownership, licenses, attribution, and revenue.
4. **Transparency:** show versions, limitations, maintenance, and support expectations.
5. **Open and commercial:** support free/open-source and paid products with clear terms.
6. **Evidence-led scope:** prioritize observed problems and transactions.
7. **Global by design, compliant by operation:** do not enable transactions where requirements cannot be met.

## What Maketrail is not (initially)

- Not a general freelance job board.
- Not a source-code hosting replacement.
- Not an unrestricted code upload and execution platform.
- Not a promise that every listing is independently audited or production-ready.
- Not a full enterprise cloud procurement marketplace at launch.

## Validation questions

- Which repeated software problems cost agencies the most time or money?
- What do buyers currently reuse, purchase, or build internally?
- What caused previous purchases or integrations to fail?
- What evidence would make a buyer trust a third-party solution?
- Which creators have products they own and are willing to maintain?
- Will buyers pay for the product, integration, or both?
- Can the marketplace earn positive contribution margin after support, payment, verification, refunds, and acquisition costs?

## Success definition for the validation stage

Proposed 30-day evidence thresholds:
- 15 agency buyer interviews.
- 20 creator interviews.
- 10 qualified creators willing to participate.
- 20 genuine product candidates.
- 5 qualified buyer requirements.
- 3 paid purchases.
- 2 successful integrations.

These are working targets, not forecasts or industry benchmarks. Missing a target should trigger diagnosis of the segment, category, pricing, trust, and transaction process—not automatic abandonment without analysis.
